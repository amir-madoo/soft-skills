# Practical Judgment and Human Capability: An Evidence-Conscious Curriculum

**Edition:** 1.0 · **Prepared:** 25 November 2026 · **Scope:** broad non-technical literacy and applied performance, not professional qualification.

## 1. Promise and boundaries

This curriculum aims to build reliable habits of inquiry, communication, collaboration, judgment, and execution across personal, organizational, civic, and commercial life. It can help a learner produce observable work and improve it through feedback. It cannot cover everything, guarantee wealth, influence, elite status, or top-percentile results, or make someone an expert in every included field. Expertise requires years of domain-specific practice, accountability, and often formal qualification or supervised work.

The design favors retrieval, spaced review, realistic practice, feedback, revision, reflection, and transfer. These are design choices supported by the learning-science literature; no single study validates this whole curriculum. Estimates are planning ranges, not promises. As of 25 November 2026, law, policy, markets, institutions, and geopolitical examples are time-sensitive and require fresh primary sources.

## 2. Scope, pace, and proficiency

**Entry assumptions:** functional reading and writing in the learner's chosen language; basic arithmetic; access to a notebook or digital journal; willingness to seek feedback; and enough numeracy to learn percentages, ratios, and charts. A learner missing these should add 40–100 hours of language/numeracy support before quantitative units. No particular degree or job is required.

**Proficiency is evidenced per domain, not awarded globally.** *Competent* means the learner can explain core concepts, produce a sound artifact in a familiar case, identify limits and ethical risks, and respond to feedback. *Advanced* means they can perform under ambiguity, compare viable approaches, adapt to a new context, quantify uncertainty where possible, and improve outcomes across multiple attempts. *Expert* means sustained, externally recognized performance in a narrow domain, sound judgment across edge cases, peer contribution, and accountable experience. This curriculum targets competence broadly and advanced performance selectively; it does not confer expertise.

**Workload:** approximately **704 hours** across the individually estimated units (credible range 620–850, depending on reading speed, feedback access, and capstone scope). That includes practice, review, and revisions embedded in units; do not add a second full review allowance on top. A realistic 6-hour/week pace is 117 weeks (about 27 months); 8 hours/week is 88 weeks (about 20 months); 3 hours/week is 235 weeks (about 4.5 years). At 10 hours/week, plan about 70 weeks with rest weeks; quality and access to feedback matter more than speed. One weekly session should be protected for review/rest. Do not turn the estimate into a deadline.

## 3. Curriculum map and dependencies

```text
Entry skills → U1 Learning & self-management ─┬→ U2 Evidence, media & information
                                               ├→ U3 Values, ethics & life design
U2 + U1 → U4 Reasoning, probability & decisions ─┬→ U5 Communication & teaching
                                                   ├→ U6 Relationships, teams & conflict
U5 + U6 → U7 Negotiation & ethical influence ─────┬→ U8 Leadership & organizations
                                                   ├→ U9 Career & professional practice
U4 + U5 → U10 Economics, finance & personal money
U4 + U10 → U11 Business, markets & entrepreneurship
U2 + U4 → U12 History, culture & geopolitics
U2 + U3 + U4 → U13 Law, governance & civic institutions
U1 + U3 + U4 + U6 → U14 Health, family, safety & civic life
U5–U14 → U15 Integration labs and five capstones
```

U1–U4 are foundational. U5–U6 can run in parallel after U1–U2. U7–U9, U10–U14 branch after prerequisites. U15 follows core units; choose one or more deepening branches. Cross-links: decision quality supports every domain; communication and ethics are practiced throughout; finance intersects career, family, business, and policy; history and institutions contextualize law and geopolitics.

## 4. How to use this curriculum

Take a baseline self-assessment and retain a sample artifact. Schedule 4–8 hours weekly in two or three sessions; reserve 20% for retrieval, review, and revisions. For each unit: read only the assigned core resource; retrieve the ideas from memory; examine a worked case; perform the guided exercise; do a transfer task in a different context; get a reviewer or use the rubric; revise once; file both drafts and a short debrief. Use local examples and record the jurisdiction/date. Revisit prompts after 1 day, 1 week, 1 month, and 3 months. Every fourth week, reduce new material and review. If a prerequisite check fails, revisit the named unit before advancing.

### Unit template and scoring

Every unit below names purpose, prerequisites, workload, objectives, concepts/debate, resources, practice, artifact, feedback/revision, rubric, review, risks, and stretch. The rubric is scored 0–3 on five dimensions: **accuracy; reasoning/evidence; execution; adaptation/transfer; ethics/limits.** 0 = absent or unsafe; 1 = partial, major errors; 2 = sound for a familiar case; 3 = robust, justified, and transferable. Pass = at least 2 on every dimension and no 0 on ethics. A reviewer should identify one strength, one specific gap, and one next attempt. Revise the artifact and annotate what changed. For units with no live access to reviewers, use a peer, supervisor, instructor, practitioner, or documented self-review against examples; disclose this limitation.

## 5. Unit specifications (15 units; approximately 704 hours including embedded practice)

### U1. Learning, attention, habits, energy, and resilience
**Domain/level:** Personal foundations · foundation. **Prereqs:** entry assumptions. **Hours:** 32. **Why:** creates a workable practice system and reduces self-deception about learning. **Objectives:** explain retrieval, spacing, feedback, and deliberate practice; build a weekly plan; track attention/energy without overclaiming causality; choose a recovery routine; revise a goal after evidence. **Concepts/debate:** working memory, retrieval, spacing, interleaving, cognitive load, motivation, habit cues, stress and recovery; effects depend on task, context, access, and health. **Resources:** Dunlosky et al., “Improving Students’ Learning With Effective Learning Techniques,” *Psychological Science in the Public Interest* (2013), https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1539-6053.2013.01054.x; National Academies, *How People Learn II* (2018), https://nap.nationalacademies.org/catalog/24783/how-people-learn-ii-learners-contexts-and-cultures.
**Guided example:** study a short unfamiliar text: predict, retrieve from memory, check, correct, repeat after delay; compare with rereading. **Independent/transfer:** run two weeks of practice on a real skill, then use the method on an unrelated skill. **Scenario:** schedule slips during a demanding work week; redesign without sacrificing sleep or care responsibilities. **Artifact:** 4-week practice and review plan plus learning log. **Assessment/feedback/revision:** rubric above, plus evidence of at least 4 retrieval attempts and revised schedule; reviewer challenges an unrealistic assumption; required second plan. **Review/link:** retrieve U1 methods at weeks 1/4/12; supports every unit. **Misconceptions/ethics:** not every difficulty is a willpower failure; protect health/privacy; avoid turning tracking into surveillance. **Stretch:** compare approaches via preregistered personal experiment, without making medical claims.

### U2. Evidence, media, information quality, and source analysis
**Domain/level:** Communication foundations · foundation. **Prereqs:** U1. **Hours:** 34. **Why:** equips the learner to distinguish evidence from confident storytelling. **Objectives:** trace a claim to its source; distinguish observation, inference, and value judgment; assess study design and data provenance; triangulate independent sources; write a calibrated summary. **Concepts/debate:** causal inference, selection effects, base rates, misinformation, expertise, uncertainty, open data, incentives; source quality is claim-specific. **Resources:** Stanford History Education Group, Civic Online Reasoning, https://cor.stanford.edu/; National Academies, *Communicating Science Effectively* (2017), https://nap.nationalacademies.org/catalog/23674/communicating-science-effectively-a-research-agenda; UNESCO, *Journalism, ‘Fake News’ & Disinformation* (2018), https://unesdoc.unesco.org/ark:/48223/pf0000265552.
**Guided:** trace one viral claim to primary materials and mark what they establish. **Independent/transfer:** audit a claim in a different medium/country; interview one knowledgeable source and verify independently. **Scenario:** a manager asks for a fast decision from a disputed chart. **Artifact:** source dossier and 700-word evidence brief with uncertainty labels. **Assessment:** rubric plus traceability, 3 independent sources including a primary/authoritative source, and explicit unknowns; peer fact-check, then revised brief. **Review/link:** source audit at 1/4/12 weeks; feeds U4, U12, U13. **Risks:** false balance, cherry-picking, quote-mining, privacy, mistaking “primary” for “unbiased.” **Stretch:** reconstruct a data series and document revisions/definitions.

### U3. Values, identity, ethics, agency, and life design
**Domain/level:** Personal foundations · foundation to applied. **Prereqs:** U1. **Hours:** 28. **Why:** aligns decisions with considered commitments while exposing trade-offs. **Objectives:** distinguish values from goals and preferences; apply at least three ethical lenses; identify conflicts of interest and affected stakeholders; create a revisable life-design hypothesis. **Concepts/debate:** consequentialism, duties/rights, virtue, care ethics, justice, autonomy, identity, plural goods; frameworks can disagree and depend on facts. **Resources:** Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy, “The History of Utilitarianism,” https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/utilitarianism-history/ and “Virtue Ethics,” https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/ethics-virtue/; Markkula Center for Applied Ethics, ethical decision-making framework, https://www.scu.edu/ethics/ethics-resources/ethical-decision-making/.
**Guided:** analyze a promotion that rewards harmful conduct through stakeholder/lens matrix. **Independent/transfer:** apply matrix to a household or civic decision. **Scenario:** values conflict with a short-term financial incentive. **Artifact:** values statement, trade-off map, and decision protocol. **Feedback/revision:** trusted reviewer tests for omitted stakeholders and hidden assumptions; revise after challenge. **Rubric:** standard rubric plus transparent trade-offs. **Review/link:** revisit at U15; connects health, career, leadership. **Risks:** values as self-justification, cultural universalizing, coercive disclosure. **Stretch:** compare two defensible decisions and identify what evidence would change the choice.

### U4. Logic, quantitative literacy, probability, and decision quality
**Domain/level:** Judgment · foundational. **Prereqs:** U1–U2; arithmetic. **Hours:** 48. **Why:** supports consequential choices under uncertainty. **Objectives:** map arguments; calculate rates and expected values; update a probability; distinguish forecast from decision; use premortem, reversibility, and postmortem; score forecast calibration. **Concepts/debate:** Bayesian updating, base rates, causal graphs, expected utility, value of information, tail risk, model limits, Goodhart effects. **Resources:** OpenIntro Statistics, free text https://www.openintro.org/book/os/; Tetlock & Gardner, *Superforecasting* (2015); UK Government Office for Science, *The Futures Toolkit* (2024 edition page), https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/futures-toolkit-for-policy-makers-and-analysts.
**Guided:** forecast a measurable event with base rate, probability, rationale, and resolution date; use a calibration/Brier score. **Independent:** forecast 10 diverse questions and update as evidence arrives. **Scenario:** choose between reversible pilot and irreversible commitment. **Artifact:** decision memo, causal map, forecast ledger, postmortem. **Feedback/revision:** peer challenges base rates and alternatives; revised forecast after new evidence. **Assessment:** correct calculations, explicit assumptions, forecast log, and decision/outcome distinction; rubric. **Review/link:** weekly forecast scoring; supports U10–U15. **Risks:** precision theater, hindsight bias, confusing good outcome with good process. **Stretch:** sensitivity analysis and reference-class forecast.

### U5. Writing, speaking, listening, teaching, and facilitation
**Domain/level:** Communication · foundation/applied. **Prereqs:** U1–U2. **Hours:** 46. **Why:** ideas only help when others can understand, question, and use them. **Objectives:** write concise evidence-backed argument; edit for audience; ask open and diagnostic questions; listen and summarize; deliver a brief presentation; facilitate disagreement; teach a concept. **Concepts/debate:** rhetoric, audience, narrative, nonverbal cues, accessibility, channel choice, cross-cultural pragmatics, digital permanence. **Resources:** Purdue OWL, https://owl.purdue.edu/; Toastmasters public speaking resources, https://www.toastmasters.org/resources/public-speaking-tips; National Academies, *How People Learn II* (2018).
**Guided:** turn a 1,000-word source dossier into a 250-word decision brief and 3-minute explanation. **Independent/transfer:** teach the same concept to an unfamiliar audience and collect comprehension feedback. **Scenario:** facilitate a hybrid meeting with unequal participation. **Artifact:** brief, talk recording/transcript, facilitation plan, revision notes. **Review:** audience comprehension test and peer rubric; revise talk and brief. **Assessment:** standard rubric plus listener can accurately restate recommendation and uncertainty. **Review/link:** practice monthly; connects U6–U9. **Risks:** confidence as proof, jargon, inaccessible formats, manipulative framing. **Stretch:** cross-cultural adaptation and plain-language versions.

### U6. Relationships, feedback, conflict, collaboration, and group dynamics
**Domain/level:** Interpersonal · applied. **Prereqs:** U3, U5. **Hours:** 42. **Why:** enables durable coordination while respecting boundaries and power differences. **Objectives:** make a specific request; give behavior-based feedback; set a boundary; diagnose conflict interests; run a team decision; identify power asymmetry and psychological safety signals. **Concepts/debate:** trust, reciprocity, status, networks, norms, groupthink, dissent, conflict styles; “psychological safety” is not comfort or lack of accountability. **Resources:** Edmondson, *The Fearless Organization* (2018); Google re:Work team effectiveness, https://rework.withgoogle.com/intl/en/guides/understand-team-effectiveness; Cialdini, *Influence* (2021 revised ed.) read critically alongside ethics framework U3.
**Guided:** use observation-impact-request feedback in a low-stakes case. **Independent:** negotiate team roles in a volunteer/project setting. **Scenario:** address repeated missed commitments where seniority is unequal. **Artifact:** team charter, conflict preparation sheet, feedback reflection. **Feedback/revision:** role-play recorded or observed; counterpart rates clarity, fairness, listening; redo with one changed tactic. **Rubric:** standard, with power awareness and repair. **Review:** debrief after each real meeting; links U7–U8. **Risks:** labeling people, forced vulnerability, retaliation, treating culture as stereotype. **Stretch:** facilitate structured dissent and decision audit.

### U7. Negotiation, sales discovery, and ethical influence
**Domain/level:** Interpersonal/business · applied to advanced practice. **Prereqs:** U4–U6. **Hours:** 48. **Why:** produces better agreements and more ethical customer conversations. **Objectives:** prepare interests, BATNA, reservation point, and questions; distinguish value creation from value claiming; conduct discovery; handle objections honestly; design implementation and contingencies; debrief outcomes and conduct. **Concepts/debate:** distributive/integrative bargaining, information asymmetry, anchoring, multi-party coalitions, cultural context, consent, negotiation research limits. **Resources:** Harvard Program on Negotiation BATNA topic, https://www.pon.harvard.edu/tag/batna/; Fisher, Ury & Patton, *Getting to Yes* (3rd ed., 2011); National Academies, *The Science of Deception* (2010), https://nap.nationalacademies.org/catalog/12882/the-science-of-deception-integrating-theory-and-practice.
**Guided:** role-play vendor contract with confidential interests; prepare, negotiate, debrief, repeat with feedback. **Independent/transfer:** salary, project scope, or customer discovery conversation. **Scenario:** cross-border supplier conflict with delivery, quality, and face-saving concerns. **Artifact:** prep sheet, agreement, debrief, ethical review. **Assessment:** role-play observation across preparation, listening, option creation, standards, clarity, ethics; counterparty and observer feedback; second negotiation required. **Review:** quarterly rehearsal; links capstone 1. **Risks:** fake BATNAs, false urgency, coercive closing, assuming cultural scripts. **Stretch:** three-party negotiation with contingent contract.

### U8. Leadership, management, organizations, and operations
**Domain/level:** Organizations · applied. **Prereqs:** U4–U6. **Hours:** 48. **Why:** individual capability must become reliable group performance. **Objectives:** delegate outcomes and authority; coach with questions; define performance evidence; map incentives and decision rights; design an operating cadence; plan change and crisis communications. **Concepts/debate:** agency, organizational structure, culture, accountability, governance, operations, motivation, principal–agent problems, change approaches; popular management prescriptions have uneven evidence and contextual limits. **Resources:** Schein, *Organizational Culture and Leadership* (5th ed., 2017); UK Cabinet Office, *The Orange Book: Management of Risk* (2023), https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/orange-book; HBR reading may supplement, not replace, empirical and local evidence.
**Guided:** diagnose delayed project using process map, incentives, and decision rights. **Independent:** redesign a small team workflow and monitor indicators. **Scenario:** service disruption and stakeholder update under incomplete facts. **Artifact:** operating model, delegation brief, risk register, after-action review. **Feedback/revision:** team member evaluates clarity and workload; revise after a two-week trial. **Rubric:** standard plus stakeholder and operational evidence. **Review:** monthly operations review; links U15 leadership capstone. **Risks:** surveillance, scapegoating, performative values, overcentralized crisis decisions. **Stretch:** governance/board memo and tabletop simulation.

### U9. Career, professional practice, and employment systems
**Domain/level:** Career · applied. **Prereqs:** U3, U5–U6. **Hours:** 30. **Why:** converts capability into credible opportunities while accounting for local labor markets. **Objectives:** map transferable skills; build evidence portfolio; conduct informational interview; tailor application; prepare compensation conversation; plan transition and manage up. **Concepts/debate:** signaling, credentials, networks, bias, labor institutions, reputation, bargaining power, personal brand limits. **Resources:** U.S. Bureau of Labor Statistics Occupational Outlook Handbook (U.S. only), https://www.bls.gov/ooh/; ILOSTAT labor data, https://ilostat.ilo.org/; local public employment service and labor ministry sources (select for jurisdiction).
**Guided:** derive accomplishment evidence from a project and tailor to two roles. **Independent:** interview three people across roles/industries; compare employment norms locally. **Scenario:** compensation discussion with uncertainty and unequal leverage. **Artifact:** portfolio, role map, interview notes, negotiation plan. **Feedback/revision:** practitioner reviews one application/interview answer; revise. **Rubric:** evidence, clarity, adaptation, ethics, local accuracy. **Review:** quarterly update. **Risks:** fabricated credentials, privacy, overpromising, assuming U.S. norms. **Stretch:** transition plan across country/sector.

### U10. Economics, finance, and personal money literacy
**Domain/level:** Economic/financial literacy · foundation/applied. **Prereqs:** U4 numeracy. **Hours:** 54. **Why:** improves understanding of incentives and personal financial choices without pretending one plan suits everyone. **Objectives:** explain supply/demand and externalities; read basic national accounts and inflation measures; compare fiscal/monetary tools; build simple budget and cash-flow model; explain diversification, compounding, insurance, credit, and taxes at a general level. **Concepts/debate:** market coordination and failures, productivity, distribution, behavioral biases, models and uncertainty; macroeconomic causal claims contested. **Resources:** OpenStax, *Principles of Economics 3e* (2024), https://openstax.org/details/books/principles-economics-3e/; Federal Reserve Education, https://www.federalreserveeducation.org/; OECD data, https://data.oecd.org/; World Bank WDI, https://databank.worldbank.org/source/world-development-indicators.
**Guided:** calculate inflation-adjusted change and compare two loan offers including fees. **Independent:** build a hypothetical household cash-flow and risk map; do not submit personal account details. **Scenario:** evaluate a policy trade-off with distributional effects. **Artifact:** economic explainer, budget model, local tax/consumer-source checklist. **Feedback/revision:** peer checks formulas and local sources; correct model. **Rubric:** arithmetic accuracy, assumptions, uncertainty, jurisdiction awareness, no personal prescriptive advice. **Review:** update indicators quarterly; links U11–U12. **Risks:** simplistic “markets always”/“government always” claims; financial product conflicts. **Stretch:** compare retirement/investment policy frameworks using official sources. Personal tax, credit, insurance, pension, and investing decisions depend on circumstances and jurisdiction; consult qualified local professionals as needed.

### U11. Business, customer value, and entrepreneurship
**Domain/level:** Business · applied. **Prereqs:** U4, U5, U7, U10. **Hours:** 52. **Why:** tests whether an organization can create value sustainably and execute. **Objectives:** conduct customer discovery; assess market and competitor evidence; define positioning; map business model and operations; calculate contribution margin and cash runway; compare funding and capital allocation; analyze failure. **Concepts/debate:** product-market fit, defensibility, scale, network effects, unit economics, brand, pricing, quality, supply chain, corporate/startup/small-business differences. **Resources:** U.S. SBA business guide (jurisdiction-specific), https://www.sba.gov/business-guide; Strategyzer Business Model Canvas, https://www.strategyzer.com/library/the-business-model-canvas; SEC company filings (U.S.), https://www.sec.gov/edgar/search/; use local registries and audited disclosures elsewhere.
**Guided:** analyze public company annual report and trace revenue, costs, cash, risks. **Independent:** interview five potential users about recent behavior, not hypothetical enthusiasm; synthesize disconfirming evidence. **Scenario:** cost shock threatens margin and service quality. **Artifact:** market evidence brief, model, operational map, 12-month financial assumptions. **Feedback/revision:** practitioner/customer critiques assumptions; revise model and note evidence changed. **Rubric:** evidence quality, coherent strategy, arithmetic, execution, ethics. **Review:** update assumptions monthly during project; capstone 2. **Risks:** survivorship bias, vanity metrics, exploiting workers/users, unsupported TAM. **Stretch:** compare organic growth, partnership, acquisition, and capital options.

### U12. History, culture, geopolitics, and strategic analysis
**Domain/level:** Global context · applied literacy. **Prereqs:** U2, U4, U10. **Hours:** 50. **Why:** puts current events in historical, geographic, economic, and institutional context. **Objectives:** build a timeline and actor map; distinguish fact/interpretation/forecast; compare regional perspectives; analyze trade/security/energy dependencies; assess competing strategic narratives; make calibrated forecasts. **Concepts/debate:** state formation, sovereignty, alliance, deterrence, development, colonial legacies, demographics, resources, migration, sanctions, international organizations, intelligence limits. **Resources:** UN Data, https://data.un.org/; World Bank WDI (above); SIPRI databases, https://www.sipri.org/databases; International Energy Agency data, https://www.iea.org/data-and-statistics; Council on Foreign Relations Global Conflict Tracker (editorial tool, not neutral primary source), https://www.cfr.org/global-conflict-tracker; one primary source per case from multiple parties.
**Guided:** build a one-page timeline and claim ledger for a current issue; label source, date, confidence, and counterevidence. **Independent:** choose another region and test whether the same framework fits. **Scenario:** supply disruption with alternative routes and stakeholder consequences. **Artifact:** 1,500-word multi-perspective brief, two scenarios, 5 scored forecasts. **Feedback/revision:** peer from a different regional background reviews framing; revise. **Rubric:** source diversity, historical context, fact/inference separation, forecast calibration, uncertainty. **Review:** revisit quarterly; capstone 3. **Risks:** civilizational stereotypes, propaganda, false equivalence, stale information. **Stretch:** scenario tree with indicators and update triggers. Date-stamp claims and re-check sources at use; as of 25 Nov 2026, forecasts and live conflict details are especially perishable.

### U13. Law, ethics, governance, and public institutions
**Domain/level:** Civic/institutional literacy · applied. **Prereqs:** U2–U4. **Hours:** 38. **Why:** consequential choices operate inside rules, rights, and institutions. **Objectives:** identify legal issue and jurisdiction; locate primary law/regulation; explain contract, employment, privacy, IP, consumer and governance basics; map policy cycle and stakeholders; analyze professional ethics and conflicts. **Concepts/debate:** rule of law, due process, enforcement capacity, rights, regulatory capture, institutional incentives, pluralism, human rights. **Resources:** World Justice Project Rule of Law Index (methodology and data), https://worldjusticeproject.org/rule-of-law-index/; OECD policy resources, https://www.oecd.org/governance/; local legislation portal, regulator, court, and ombudsman sources (must be selected for jurisdiction).
**Guided:** trace a consumer privacy issue from statute to regulator guidance to enforcement example. **Independent:** compare a workplace or contract rule in two jurisdictions from primary sources. **Scenario:** organization receives a data request that conflicts with privacy commitments. **Artifact:** jurisdiction-aware issue tree and civic policy memo, clearly marked educational not legal advice. **Feedback/revision:** local legal educator/practitioner checks source path; revise. **Rubric:** jurisdiction/source accuracy, distinction between law and ethics, stakeholders, limits. **Review:** recheck before any live decision; links U8/U11. **Risks:** unauthorized legal advice, outdated law, assuming text equals enforcement. **Stretch:** compare policy alternatives and implementation incentives. Obtain qualified legal counsel for actual disputes, contracts, tax, immigration, or compliance decisions.

### U14. Health literacy, family/care, consumer, environment, and personal safety
**Domain/level:** Life and civic capability · applied literacy. **Prereqs:** U1, U3–U4, U6. **Hours:** 36. **Why:** omitted daily systems can dominate well-being and decision quality. **Objectives:** assess health claims using authoritative sources; prepare questions for clinicians; map caregiving load and consent; compare consumer total cost and warranties; identify environmental externalities; make a personal safety plan appropriate to local context; identify credible public-health guidance. **Concepts/debate:** prevention, risk communication, social determinants, care work, consumer protection, environmental justice, privacy and safety trade-offs. **Resources:** WHO health topics, https://www.who.int/health-topics; WHO risk communication guidance, https://www.who.int/teams/risk-communication; UNEP resources, https://www.unep.org/resources; local public-health agency, poison control/emergency service, consumer regulator, and environmental data portal.
**Guided:** compare a health claim with systematic guidance and prepare questions, not self-diagnosis. **Independent:** evaluate total ownership cost and consumer rights for a significant purchase in local context. **Scenario:** caregiver faces conflicting obligations and needs support. **Artifact:** household resilience map (no sensitive data), clinician question list, consumer comparison, localized emergency contacts. **Feedback/revision:** trusted adult/professional validates local resources; revise. **Rubric:** source authority, uncertainty, privacy, feasibility, appropriate referral. **Review:** refresh contacts and plans twice a year. **Risks:** medical/legal overreach, victim-blaming, unsafe disclosure, inequity. **Stretch:** public-health or climate adaptation case study. This unit provides literacy, not medical, legal, or emergency-response qualification.

### U15. Integration studios and five capstones
**Domain/level:** Synthesis · advanced practice. **Prereqs:** U1–U14 (selective branches accepted if gaps declared). **Hours:** 118 (five capstones averaging 18–28 hours, plus reviews). **Why:** demonstrates transfer through consequential but bounded work. **Objectives:** integrate evidence, values, quantitative reasoning, communication, and feedback; deliver a real or realistic outcome; reflect on errors; revise. **Concepts:** systems, stakeholder effects, implementation, ethical constraints, uncertainty, learning loops. **Resources:** relevant unit resources and local primary sources; no one reading substitutes for field evidence.

**Capstone 1 — Negotiation (22h):** choose workplace, vendor, community, or salary case; do not use confidential real data. Milestones: scope/consent and context; interests/BATNAs/standards; role-play or supervised live discussion; agreement review; debrief and revised second attempt. Evidence: prep sheet, observer notes, terms, ethical review. Reviewers: counterpart plus neutral observer. Rubric: prep, listening, value creation, clarity, implementation, ethics (0–3 each; ethics must be ≥2). Incomplete if no alternative analysis, feedback, or revised attempt.

**Capstone 2 — Business analysis or validated concept (26h):** analyze a real organization using public data, or test a small concept ethically. Milestones: question; customer/market evidence; strategy and competition; operating model; basic financial model; critique and revision. Evidence: 5+ behavior-focused interviews or transparent public sources; assumptions and unit economics; risk/ethics review. Reviewers: prospective user and business/accounting-informed reader. Incomplete if claims lack traceable evidence or cash/unit economics are absent.

**Capstone 3 — Economics/geopolitics current issue (24h):** select a live issue; timestamp all claims and define a 6–18 month forecast horizon. Milestones: source map; historical/context memo; competing interpretations; scenarios and indicators; forecasts with resolution rules; review. Evidence: primary sources from at least two parties/regions plus independent authoritative data; 5 forecasts with probabilities. Reviewer: peer with different background plus source checker. Incomplete if facts and inference blur, sources are single-perspective, or forecasts cannot resolve.

**Capstone 4 — Leadership and communication group outcome (22h):** lead or facilitate a bounded group project with consent and a real output. Milestones: charter and baseline; decision process; check-in; deliverable; feedback from at least two participants; reflection and revised approach. Evidence: work plan, meeting artifact, outcome measure, feedback, revision. Reviewers: participants. Incomplete if no participant feedback, outcome evidence, or learning-based change.

**Capstone 5 — Personal strategy (24h):** integrate values, career, finances, relationships, health, time, and uncertainty into a 1–3 year revisable plan. Milestones: values and constraints; current-state map; scenarios; experiments and stop rules; review with trusted person; revision. Evidence: non-sensitive summary, assumptions, local financial/health/legal referrals where needed. Reviewer: trusted person of learner's choice; disclose only what learner chooses. Rubric: coherence, realism, flexibility, ethical commitments, care and uncertainty; no score for income/status. Incomplete if plan assumes mechanical optimization or lacks contingencies and revision.

**Capstone assessment:** standard five-dimension rubric plus the capstone criteria. Each gets written feedback, one revision, and a short postmortem separating outcome quality from decision process. Learner can mark a project incomplete if context, access, consent, or safety makes it unsuitable; substitute a public case or simulation and state the limitation. Keep a portfolio with versions, feedback, and redacted evidence.

## 6. Practice, tracking, and feedback loop

Maintain a learning ledger with date, objective, retrieval score, practice repetitions, feedback received, revision, transfer test, confidence, and next review. Monthly review: select one persistent error; one piece of counterevidence; one revised plan. Forecast ledger records question, probability, rationale, update, resolution rule/date, outcome, Brier score (for binary questions, mean of (p−outcome)^2), and calibration by probability band after at least 30 resolved forecasts. Do not over-interpret small samples. Decision journal records options, assumptions, expected outcomes, reversibility, stakeholders, and post-outcome process review. Quarterly artifact audit: compare early and recent work blindly if feasible. A failed attempt is useful when the learner can describe what changed in the next attempt.

## 7. Branches after shared core

Branches are emphasis, not credentials. Add 80–200 hours each, with supervised field practice where possible.

- **Founder/small business:** prioritize U7, U10–U11, U13; add customer discovery repetitions, bookkeeping, local registration/tax/compliance instruction, and a mentor review of cash model.
- **Executive/manager:** prioritize U6–U8, U13; add labor law/local governance, hiring/performance practice, operations simulation, and multi-rater feedback.
- **Negotiator/sales:** prioritize U5–U7, U13; complete 20+ role-plays across distributive/integrative and multi-party settings; seek coached observation; specialize in an industry.
- **Policy/geopolitics:** prioritize U2, U4, U10, U12–U13; add regional history/language and methods training; develop source networks across perspectives; practice forecast resolution.
- **Financially capable generalist:** prioritize U4, U9–U11, U13–U14; localize tax, pension, insurance, consumer credit; use licensed fiduciary/tax professionals for personal decisions. No portfolio prescription is made here.
